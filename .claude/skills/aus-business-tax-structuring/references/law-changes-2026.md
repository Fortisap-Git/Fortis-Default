# Law changes and current rates, as at September 2026

Status labels: **LAW** (enacted), **ED** (exposure draft released), **ANNOUNCED** (Budget measure, no draft yet). Anything not LAW must be labelled as such in advice. Re-verify with a search before quoting; this file is a snapshot.

## 2026-27 Federal Budget (12 May 2026): the structuring measures

### CGT discount replaced by indexation plus a 30 per cent minimum tax (ANNOUNCED, from 1 July 2027)

- The 50 per cent CGT discount is removed for gains accruing on or after 1 July 2027 and replaced by CPI indexation of the cost base (assets held at least 12 months), in the manner of the 1985 to 1999 rules.
- A minimum tax of 30 per cent applies to net capital gains after indexation. Mechanically it compares 30 per cent of the indexed gain with the tax actually payable on it at the taxpayer's rates and collects the gap. Income support recipients are exempt.
- Applies to CGT assets held by individuals, trusts and partnerships. Companies are already outside the discount and are not changed. Complying super funds are not in scope.
- Pre-CGT assets come into the net for gains accruing from 1 July 2027, with a market value cost base at that date.
- Transitional: gains accrued to 30 June 2027 keep the 50 per cent discount when the asset is later sold; indexation and the minimum tax apply to the gain after that date. The apportionment method is not yet settled, so advise clients to hold a market valuation as at 30 June 2027 for appreciating assets.
- New residential property: investors may elect between the old discount and the new regime on disposal.
- Main residence exemption is untouched.

Structuring effect: from 1 July 2027 an individual pays at least 30 per cent on real gains; a company pays 30 per cent on nominal gains (25 per cent if a base rate entity) with no indexation; a discretionary trust passes gains to beneficiaries under the same minimum. The gap between own name and company narrows for gains but own name keeps indexation and the company does not. Crystallising a gain before 1 July 2027 to "bank" the discount is not needed, because the accrued gain keeps the discount anyway.

### Minimum tax on discretionary trusts (ED released 3 September 2026, consultation to 18 September 2026, from 1 July 2028)

- A 30 per cent minimum tax on the net income of in-scope discretionary trusts, imposed at trustee level.
- Individual (non-corporate) beneficiaries presently entitled get a non-refundable credit for the trustee tax on their share. Corporate beneficiaries get no credit, so distributing to a bucket company no longer caps tax at 25 to 30 per cent; it stacks.
- Excluded: fixed trusts and widely held trusts, complying superannuation funds, charitable trusts, special disability trusts, deceased estates.
- Excluded election trust (EET): the draft lets a trustee elect to nominate the beneficiaries and their fixed shares of income and capital (corporate beneficiaries allowed), which takes the trust out of the minimum tax without a restructure and, Treasury says, without state duty. Treat the duty point as unconfirmed until the revenue offices say so.
- Restructure rollover: three years from 1 July 2027 to move out of a discretionary trust into a company or fixed trust with no income tax or CGT consequence. Completing the restructure in 2027-28 avoids the minimum tax entirely. The Commonwealth cannot relieve state duty; every state's duty position must be checked separately and none has announced relief.

Structuring effect: every Fortis client with a business or property in a discretionary trust needs a decision during 2026-27 and 2027-28: stay and pay the minimum with credits to individuals, elect EET, or roll into a company or unit trust. The trust plus bucket company strategy in the old version of this skill no longer works after 30 June 2028. New property acquisitions should default to own name, a company or a unit trust unless the discretionary trust has a stated non-tax reason.

### Negative gearing limited for established dwellings bought after Budget night (ANNOUNCED, from 1 July 2027)

- Net rental losses on residential dwellings acquired after 7.30pm on 12 May 2026, other than new builds, may only be deducted against income from residential dwellings from 1 July 2027, with unused losses carried forward.
- Dwellings acquired before 12 May 2026 are grandfathered while held by the same owner. New residential dwellings are exempt.
- A transfer of an existing dwelling into a new entity is an acquisition by that entity after Budget night, so the new owner is inside the quarantine from 1 July 2027 even though the individual was grandfathered.

### Other Budget measures relevant to SMB structuring

- Company tax rates unchanged: 25 per cent base rate entity (aggregated turnover under $50m and no more than 80 per cent passive income), otherwise 30 per cent. **LAW**
- $20,000 instant asset write-off made permanent for small business entities (turnover under $10m) from 1 July 2026. **LAW**
- Loss carry-back for eligible companies from 2026-27 against tax paid in the prior two years. **ANNOUNCED** (check enactment).
- Small business CGT concessions (Div 152) preserved.

## Superannuation

- Division 296: additional 15 per cent on earnings attributable to balances between $3m and $10m and an additional 25 per cent above $10m, on realised earnings, from 1 July 2026 (first assessment for 2026-27). Thresholds indexed. **LAW** (passed 10 March 2026).
- Superannuation guarantee 12 per cent. **LAW**
- Concessional cap $32,500 from 1 July 2026; general transfer balance cap $2.1m from 1 July 2026. **LAW** (indexation)
- Carry-forward concessional contributions still available where total super balance under $500,000.

## Individuals, 2026-27 resident rates (LAW)

| Taxable income | Rate |
|---|---|
| $0 to $18,200 | nil |
| $18,201 to $45,000 | 15 per cent (down from 16) |
| $45,001 to $135,000 | 30 per cent |
| $135,001 to $190,000 | 37 per cent |
| over $190,000 | 45 per cent |

Plus Medicare levy 2 per cent. The 15 per cent rate falls to 14 per cent from 1 July 2027 (legislated).

## Division 7A

Benchmark interest rate 8.77 per cent for 2026-27 (8.37 per cent for 2025-26; 8.27 per cent for 2024-25 and 2023-24). Seven year unsecured, 25 year secured by registered mortgage. Minimum yearly repayment due by 30 June. Loan agreement in place before the company's lodgement day. Deemed dividends are unfranked.

## CGT, foreign residents

- Foreign resident capital gains withholding 15 per cent on all Australian real property contracts from 1 January 2025, no value threshold. Australian resident vendors need a clearance certificate on every sale. **LAW**
- Foreign resident CGT reform Bill introduced July 2026: broader "real property" definition, principal asset test changes, from as early as 1 October 2026, prospective only. **BILL** (check passage).

## GST

- Registration threshold $75,000 (unchanged).
- GSTR 2012/6 (commercial residential premises) and GSTR 2012/7 (long term accommodation) remain the operative rulings for boarding and rooming houses; a draft update for build-to-rent was released in 2025.

## State taxes: headline changes to note

- NSW: land tax thresholds frozen (general $1,075,000, premium $6,571,000 for 2026); foreign owner land tax surcharge 5 per cent; surcharge purchaser duty 9 per cent; premium transfer duty threshold $3,870,000 for 2026-27; annual boarding house and low cost accommodation land tax rulings issued for 2026.
- VIC: general land tax threshold $50,000 with the COVID debt surcharge to 2033; trust surcharge from $25,000; absentee owner surcharge 4 per cent; payroll tax threshold $1m.
- QLD: land tax thresholds $600,000 individuals and $350,000 companies, trustees and absentees, unchanged; AFAD 8 per cent; from 1 August 2026 home concessions limited to citizens, permanent residents and specified retirees.
- ACT payroll tax threshold $1.75m (rate 6.75 per cent entry), NT $2.5m.
