# Division 7A (ITAA 1936 ss 109B to 109ZE)

A private company that pays, lends or forgives an amount to a shareholder or an associate is treated as having paid an **unfranked** dividend up to its distributable surplus, unless an exception applies. In a restructure the two places it appears are: the company's existing loan account with the director, and any refinance proceeds that flow from a new property company to the individual.

## Complying loan (s 109N)

1. Written agreement before the company's lodgement day for the year the loan is made.
2. Interest at or above the benchmark rate for each year after the year the loan is made.
3. Maximum term seven years unsecured, 25 years secured by a registered mortgage over real property with the loan not exceeding 91% of the property's value.
4. Minimum yearly repayment by 30 June of each year after the loan year: MYR = balance × r / (1 − (1 + r)^−n), with r the benchmark rate and n the years remaining. Shortfall is a deemed dividend.

## Trust UPEs to a corporate beneficiary

A UPE that the trust keeps using is a loan for Div 7A purposes from the time the company is taken to have consented to the trust retaining it (TD 2022/11; the ATO's post-Bendel position is that a UPE can still be a "financial accommodation" and the matter should be treated as live). Put it on a complying loan or pay it out. Note that from 1 July 2028 (announced) distributions to corporate beneficiaries from a discretionary trust get no credit for the trustee minimum tax, so the UPE strategy loses its purpose anyway.

## Restructure interactions

- **Property company borrows more than it pays the individual.** The excess is company money. Paying it to the shareholder is a Div 7A loan unless it is salary or a dividend. Build the refinance so the company borrows the price and its costs, no more, or document a complying loan.
- **Individual sells property to the company for less than market value.** CGT still at market value (s 116-30) and duty at market value; the shortfall is a contribution to the company's capital, not a Div 7A issue, but it moves value into the shares.
- **Existing director loan in the trading company.** Clear or formalise it before any new structure is layered on; a deemed dividend in the restructure year is the most common own goal.
- **Franking.** A properly declared franked dividend to clear a loan gives the shareholder a 30% (or 25%) credit; a deemed dividend gives none. Always prefer the declaration.

## Repayment strategies

| Strategy | Mechanics | Watch |
|---|---|---|
| Franked dividend set off against the loan | Declare before lodgement day, journal against the loan | Needs distributable surplus and franking credits; top-up tax at the shareholder's marginal rate |
| Salary or bonus | PAYG withheld, after-tax amount repays | Super guarantee 12% on salary; payroll tax if grouped over threshold |
| Cash repayment | From personal funds before lodgement day | Repayments funded by a fresh loan from the company are disregarded (s 109R) |
| Complying loan agreement | Seven or 25 years at benchmark | MYR every 30 June; interest is assessable to the company |

## Deadlines

| Event | Deadline |
|---|---|
| Written loan agreement | Before the company's lodgement day (due date or actual lodgement, whichever first) |
| Minimum yearly repayment | 30 June each year |
| UPE decision (sub-trust or loan) | Before the corporate beneficiary's lodgement day for the year the UPE arose |
