# Holding property: own name, company, discretionary trust, unit trust, SMSF

The comparison after the 2026-27 Budget. Use it to answer "company or trust?" for an investment property and to test a restructure against staying put. Every row is a live consideration; put the ones that carry dollars into the model.

## 2. Reading the table for a single investment property

**Own name is the default for a leveraged residential rental** while the owner can use the losses, gets the discount or indexation, and sits under the land tax threshold. The two things that move a client out of own name are asset protection from a risky business or profession, and a genuine lending advantage that has been tested in the market.

**A company suits** positively geared property when the owner is on 47% and wants to accumulate, accepts 30% on the eventual sale with no indexation, and has no need to draw the surplus personally. It is the wrong home for a negatively geared asset and the wrong home for a family that expects to sell in a few years.

**A discretionary trust for property is now a special case.** From 1 July 2028 the minimum tax removes the income splitting advantage unless the beneficiaries are all individuals on 30% or more anyway, the bucket company strategy stops working, and NSW and VIC land tax penalise it every year. Use it where asset protection or succession is the stated reason, or where the client already has one with property in it and the restructure rollover (1 July 2027 to 30 June 2030) is being weighed against the duty cost of moving.

**A fixed unit trust** is the entity for co-investors and, after the Budget, the natural replacement for a discretionary trust holding property: excluded from the trust minimum tax, indexation and discount transition flow to unitholders, NSW general land tax threshold if it meets the fixed trust definition. Units are exposed to the unitholder's creditors and losses are trapped.

**An SMSF** is for business real property leased to the owner's business, or for cash-funded residential property acquired at arm's length. It cannot buy residential property from a member or related party.

## 3. Restructure or refinance: the test

A restructure that is driven by lending has to pass this in the model:

1. Upfront cost: CGT on the transfer, duty, valuation, legal and conveyancing, loan break and establishment costs, entity set up.
2. Annual cost: extra land tax at the new owner's threshold, extra compliance, lost negative gearing (if the client's other income currently absorbs losses), Div 7A friction if a company.
3. Annual benefit: the interest saving on the actual new balance at the confirmed new rate, less the interest on any extra borrowing.
4. Payback in years. Anything beyond seven to ten years, or that depends on a rate differential the lender has not confirmed in writing, is a no.
5. The alternative: the same rate in the client's own name with another lender, or a lender that prices the asset type (rooming houses, dual occupancy, NDIS) properly. Brokers quoting a lower rate for an entity borrower are usually pricing a different product; the product may be available to the individual too.

Also check who receives the refinance proceeds. If the entity borrows more than it pays the individual for the property, the excess is entity cash and taking it personally from a company is a Div 7A loan. If the entity borrows less than the price, the individual is vendor financing the entity; document the loan and decide on interest.

## 4. GST for rooming and boarding houses

Residential premises let to tenants are input taxed: no GST on rent, no input tax credits, no GST on sale. Commercial residential premises (hotel, motel, inn, hostel, boarding house and anything similar) are taxable: GST on accommodation (with a concession for stays over 28 days under GSTR 2012/7, or an election to treat long term supplies as input taxed), input tax credits on costs, and GST on the sale of the premises unless a going concern.

GSTR 2012/6 lists the features that point to commercial residential premises: commercial intention, multiple occupancy, holding out to the public, accommodation as the main purpose, central management, management offering accommodation in its own right, services and facilities provided, occupants with the status of guests. A five room rooming house with furnished rooms, shared kitchen and laundry, individual rooming agreements under the state tenancy Act and an agent managing lettings sits near the line. If it is commercial residential premises and gross accommodation income exceeds $75,000, the owner should be registered; a cancelled ABN on such an owner is a red flag. Refer the classification to tax-guru for a cited answer before advising. The classification also drives the duty base on any transfer (GST-inclusive consideration).

## 5. Interest deductibility after a transfer

The new entity's interest is deductible where the borrowing funds the acquisition of the income-producing property (the purchase from the individual) and refinancing of that debt. Interest on borrowings used to pay the individual more than the property is worth, or to fund private outgoings, is not. Trace every dollar of the new facility to a purpose and record it. In a company, interest on money lent on to a shareholder is deductible only if the on-loan is at arm's length interest.

## 6. Dormant entities: what to do with them

Every group audit turns up an idle trust or company. Deal with them in the same advice.

| Situation | Options | Cost and steps |
|---|---|---|
| Discretionary trust settled, never used, no ABN or TFN | (a) Keep as a shelf entity for a stated purpose with a diary note; (b) vest and wind up under the deed | (a) No lodgement while no income; confirm no TFN was ever issued, otherwise lodge a non-lodgement advice. Review the deed for foreign beneficiary exclusion and appointor succession while it sits. (b) Trustee resolution to vest, distribute the settled sum, deed of vesting if required; small legal cost. After 2028 a discretionary trust is rarely the vehicle for new property, so do not keep it "for the next purchase" without a reason |
| Company registered, never traded | (a) Keep as a future trustee or holding company; (b) voluntary deregistration | (a) ASIC annual review fee continues (about $320 a year for a proprietary company), nil tax return if genuinely dormant with a return not necessary lodged. (b) ASIC Form 6010, $49 fee, requires no assets over $1,000, no liabilities, all fees paid, members agree |
| Company with retained earnings and Div 7A loan to the director | Clear the Div 7A position before any restructure | Franked dividend to offset, or complying loan agreement with minimum yearly repayments at 8.77%; document before lodgement day |
| Trust with a corporate beneficiary UPE | Sub-trust or complying loan | PCG 2017/13 and TD 2022/11; and plan for the 2028 minimum tax, under which future corporate beneficiary distributions get no credit |

## 7. Business structures, briefly

The business side of the old skill still holds with two amendments. First, the discretionary trust plus bucket company model ends on 30 June 2028 under the announced minimum tax; the working defaults for a trading business become an operating company owned by a holding company or by individuals, or a fixed unit trust where there are co-owners, with a discretionary trust only where asset protection and succession justify it and the EET election is available. Second, the three year rollover from 1 July 2027 is the window to move an existing trading trust into a company without income tax cost; duty on business assets (goodwill is not dutiable in most states now, land is) has to be checked per state.
